## A citation and its row

An answer cites its sources by number in its words, and lists them under it in Sources. The page holds two things: which source is under the pointer or holds focus, and whether the list is open.

- Resting on a citation, or tabbing to it, lights the row it stands for. Resting on a row, or tabbing to it, lights every citation of it.
- A press on a citation opens the list and goes to its row: the row's link takes focus and is scrolled into view.

A citation is a link to its row, named for what it cites ("Source 1: Churn by region, Q3"), so a screen reader hears where it leads. It is drawn as the original design drew it, from tokens: a small box on the soft fill (`muted`) with the system's edge, its number in the quiet ink. Lit, the citation and the row's box take the highlight fill (`accent-highlight`) and the number takes the ink; the list's own rows stay its items.

## What it is made of

- **Sources**, held open by the page (`open`, `onOpenChange`), with a **Source** for each row.
- A link of the pattern's own for each citation in the words, with the library's focus ring and a finger's target (`focusRing`, `tapTarget`).
- Two pieces of state for the light, the source under the pointer and the one holding focus, so a pointer leaving never puts out the light that focus holds.

Where a citation should show the source itself in a card, on hover and on focus, use [Inline citation](/components/inline-citation) in the words and keep Sources under the answer for a touch screen and a screen reader.
