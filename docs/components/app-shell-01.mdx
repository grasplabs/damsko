A block is a ready-made composition of components that a product installs, then owns and edits. It is not a primitive.

## Basic usage

`sidebar` takes the product's Sidebar parts under the sidebar's head, which the shell draws: the product's `mark` at its start, then a search button when the shell has `onSearch`, then the fold. The search opens the product's own dialog, from its button and from Cmd+K or Ctrl+K anywhere on the page (`searchShortcut` changes the keys, `false` turns them off); never in rich text, where the same keys shape the text. This site keeps Cmd+K for its own search, so here the button opens the example's. A CommandDialog as the product's search takes `shortcut={null}`: the shell holds the keys. The page stands beside the sidebar as a card and scrolls on its own.

Each page draws its own bar, AppShellHeader, at its top, so a router's page says where it is: `breadcrumb` is the steps before the page, each a name and its `href`, or a router's link as `render` (`render: <Link to="/projects" />`); `title` is the page itself; the bar writes the slash between every two. With `onRename` a press on the page's name, Enter or F2 renames it in place, and `renaming` opens it from elsewhere, such as a menu's Rename. `trailing` stands after the path and gives way to the `actions` at its end. The fold stands in the bar only while the sidebar is away, folded or in its sheet; while it is open, the fold is in the sidebar's head, and focus follows it there when the bar's fold opens the sidebar. Under the bar, AppShellPage is the page's body: as wide as the card, its sides in the page's room, its parts a step apart.

## Kept as the person left it

`storageKey` keeps whether the sidebar is folded, and which of its SidebarCollapsible groups are, in localStorage, so the next visit opens the frame as the person left it. Put `sidebarStateScript(storageKey)` in the page's head (or, as the basic example does, just before the shell): it draws the kept fold before the page wakes, and the bar's fold, drawn by the server too, shows from the sidebar's state alone, so nothing slides or appears as the page wakes. Or keep it yourself with `open` and `onOpenChange` (and the sheet's with `openMobile` and `onOpenMobileChange`); `defaultOpen` and `keyboardShortcut` are SidebarProvider's.

## Composed by hand

AppShell is its parts composed for a frame of its own. A product that lays more round the page, such as a chat beside it or a dock over its foot, composes the same parts inside its own SidebarProvider: AppShellSidebar (the sidebar under its head), then AppShellCard (the page's card, the main landmark) wherever its own layout puts it, holding AppShellContent (the page's scroller). The card's `overlay` stands over it under the banner and is never covered, for a product's own buttons at the bar's height; the bar keeps room for them at its end when an element round the page sets `appShellVars.barEndRoom`. With `covered`, a panel lies over the whole card: the banner and the page fade out and go out of reach, and the overlay stays.

## A panel at the end

`panel` holds one Side panel at the page's end, seated unframed, from the card's top beside the page's bar. Drag its handle or move it with the arrow keys; the bar's panel button hides and shows it. On a narrow window it opens as a sheet from the end, which holds Tab inside and gives focus back to the button when it closes. Keep it with `panelOpen` and `onPanelOpenChange`.

## A banner

`banner` stands above the page and its bar, across the card: a notice for the whole product, on every page.

## Levels

An area with a navigation of its own takes the sidebar while the person is in it: `sidebar` holds Sidebar's SidebarLevels, and the address says which level is shown. Under `settings/` here it is Settings' own list, with the way back to the page that was left.

## The sidebar's parts

`app-shell-01-sidebar` holds the parts a product's sidebar is made of, drawn with Sidebar's own. AppShellSwitch stands under the head and switches the main list's views (Home | Inbox): a track whose chosen tab rides on a thumb that glides from tab to tab, one tab stop, the arrows, Home and End moving and choosing; a tab's count is a red dot on its icon and is said in its name, and again when it changes. Its children are the rows it switches, which fade from one list to the next; without children it is a level's views of the page.

AppShellNav takes the rows as data, in groups, each row and group with an `id` of its own (two rows may share a name): a group has a small label, a count beside it, folds away (kept under the shell's `storageKey`), may be a landmark of its own and end in a quiet way to all of them. A row is a link (`href`, or a router's link as `render`) or a button, with its icon; at its end a count (said as the number, or `countText`), short words (`end`, said as `endText`), the Working mark or an unread dot, each said in words; a second line (`detail`), the row growing to hold it; its name in the regular weight for a thing in a list of them (`quiet`); and `endAction`, a control of the product's own such as a mark that leads elsewhere. With `actions` its menu stands behind three dots at its end and on a right-click, a long press, the menu key or Shift+F10; with `onRename` the menu's entry marked `rename`, F2 or a double-click lays a field over its words, which Enter or leaving saves and Escape leaves. A row that acts closes the sidebar's sheet, as a link followed does. An AppShellNavEmpty says one quiet sentence where there are no rows.

A level's head is Sidebar's own SidebarLevelHeader: Back, its chevron alone and named, and the level's name, a link to its own first page with `asChild`, as one raised row. AppShellProfile is the person at the foot: their face, their name and one quiet line, and their menu over the row, as wide as it, of presses, a submenu of choices with a tick, the theme as icon segments at the end of its row the arrows choose, and lines between. The rows' words (the dots' name, the rename field's, the hint, Working and Unread) come from the I18nProvider's `appShell`.

## Loading, not there, failed

A page keeps its bar in every state, and with it the fold on a phone. While a slow read comes, AppShellPageSkeleton draws the pages' usual layout as skeletons and says once that it is loading. A page that is not there, or did not load, is an Empty in the AppShellPage that grows (`flexGrow: 1`) into the card's middle, with the way on.

<Stage path="app-shell-01/states" label="Loading, not there, failed" />

## On a phone

Under the lg breakpoint the sidebar is Sidebar's own sheet from the start, opened by the bar's fold; its head holds a close button in place of the fold, and following a link in it closes it. Under 640px the bar says the page and the step before it, a slash between, and drops the step when both do not fit.

## Its words

What the shell says itself (the search button's name, the sheet's close button, the panel's button and handle, the rename field, "Loading…") comes from the I18nProvider's `appShell` part, in the product's language, for AppShell and for each part used alone.
