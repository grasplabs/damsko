## Writing and sending

Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a line; Enter while an input method composes, or while a menu is open, does neither. Once sent, `status` follows the answer: Submit turns into Stop while it streams, and `onStop` ends it.

<Stage path="prompt-input/basic" label="Writing and sending" />

## Send and Stop

Submit by `status`: off while there is nothing to send, Send once there is, a spinner once `submitted`, Stop while `streaming`, and Send again, its arrow unchanged, after an `error`, which the band under the card says.

<Stage path="prompt-input/send" label="Send and Stop" />

## While the answer comes

While `status` is `submitted` or `streaming` a soft light travels along the card's top edge: three beams at three speeds, still with reduced motion and absent in forced colours. `PromptInputNote` is the band tucked under the card's foot; give it what the assistant is doing now, an `AgentIndicator` and a `Shimmer` of the product's words. With nothing to say there is no band.

<Stage path="prompt-input/working" label="While the answer comes" />

## An accent

`accent` colours the working light and the success halo: the brand's light by default, or one colour, such as an assistant's own.

<Stage path="prompt-input/accents" label="An accent" />

## Success

`status="success"` is a moment the product marks: one halo round the card, in the accent, said once ("Done"), and Submit as at `ready`. Set it again to play it again. With reduced motion the halo stands still for that moment, with no movement; in forced colours there is none, only the words.

<Stage path="prompt-input/success" label="Success" />

## Mentions and commands

`PromptInputMention` takes the characters that open a menu, each with its items or a loader and the token a pick becomes. A pick is a token in the text: it moves, deletes and copies as one piece, and is said with its kind. A loader that fails says so in the menu; an answer that comes after a newer query is set aside.

The mention part is generic: it takes `triggers`, a list of characters with what each one's menu holds, so `@` for people, `/` for commands and `#` for channels are one part with one API, and it knows none of them.

A trigger with `kinds` opens on a first level: the kinds of thing it suggests, each with a line of help and a chevron. Picking a kind keeps the menu open on the things of that kind (those whose `group` is the kind's `label`); typing at the first level searches every kind at once.

<Stage path="prompt-input/mentions" label="Mentions and commands" />

## Files

The prompt input keeps the files: picked with Add files (or Cmd or Ctrl and /), pasted into the text or dropped on the card (with `globalDrop`, anywhere on the page), and only those `accept` takes. As soon as a file is dragged over the page the card is tinted and says "Drop to add"; over the card the tint deepens. A paste that holds a file and words keeps both. `PromptInputAttachments` in `PromptInputHeader` shows them with the `attachment` parts, each one's remove button taking it off; they go to `onSubmit` with the text as `files`, and are taken off once the send succeeds. Files alone are nothing to send: Submit stays off until there are words. `usePromptInputAttachments()` reads and changes them from a part of your own.

<Stage path="prompt-input/attachments" label="Files" />

### Files that upload as they are attached

A product that uploads each file as it is attached draws it with `PromptInputAttachment` and Attachment's own `state`, with a `description` in place of its size: `uploading` (quiet and busy, how far in its line) or `error` (in the destructive colour, with the reason, which is said once). While a file is on its way or refused nothing is sent: Submit is off, and Enter sends nothing, until every file is there or taken off with its remove action, which each file keeps in every state.

## A limit

With `maxLength`, a count shows from 90%; nearing and reaching the limit are said. What is added past it at once, a paste, a drop, an input method's phrase, a dictation or a mention, is cut there where it went (a mention whole or not at all), and that is said.

<Stage path="prompt-input/limit" label="A limit" />

## A send that fails

Return a Promise from `onSubmit` that settles when the message is taken, and the words and files stay where they are until it resolves; then what was sent clears, and words typed meanwhile stay. (The AI SDK's `sendMessage` settles only once the whole answer has come: do not return that one, or return nothing, and they clear at once.) When the Promise rejects, or `status` is `error`, nothing was lost: the failure is said once, `PromptInputNote` shows it on the band under the card, in the catalogue's words (`promptInput.failed`), and the text is described by it. The band goes as the person types on, or sends again. While a send is out, a second Enter sends nothing more.

<Stage path="prompt-input/failed" label="A send that fails" />

## One row

`compact` sets the tools, the text and Submit side by side, 56px tall at every width. Once the words no longer fit their line, or the room beside the tools is under `prompt-input-field-min`, the text takes the card's whole width and the tools and Submit go under it; shorter again, it is one row again.

<Stage path="prompt-input/compact" label="One row" />

## On glass

`variant="glass"` is for a card the page scrolls behind, as at a chat's foot: the card's colour most of the way, over a blur of what is behind it.

<Stage path="prompt-input/glass" label="On glass" />

## A banner above the card

`PromptInputBanner` is a notice that belongs to the prompt input, tucked under the card's top: a quiet strip with an [Alert](/components/alert) in it, a mark, one short line, one `action` at its end (under the words on a narrow card) and, with `onClose`, a close button, which gives the focus to the text. It is said as an Alert is: a `warning` or `destructive` one at once as it appears, a `default` one where it stands. The strip is the same for every `variant`: the mark takes the colour. Use it for what stops or limits writing here: a limit reached, something to connect first, a microphone that is blocked.

The banner, the queue and the note stand outside the card, so the PromptInput looks for them among its own children: write them straight in it (a Fragment or a list is fine), not inside a component of your own, where they are not drawn and a development build says so on the console.

<Stage path="prompt-input/banner" label="A banner above the card" />

## Queued messages

`PromptInputQueue` is the panel above the card, its foot behind the card, for the messages written while an answer was still coming: its `label` on a fold ("2 queued"), and a `PromptInputQueueItem` for each, with its `actions` at the row's end. The product keeps the queue and what each action does. When an action takes its row off from the keyboard, focus goes on to the next row's first action, or the one before, and after the last row to the text.

<Stage path="prompt-input/queue" label="Queued messages" />

## While the page loads

With `loading` the prompt input stands still in the box it will have: the plus, a quiet line where the text goes and Submit off; its other tools keep their room, not drawn. Nothing in it is focused or read out, so the page says that it loads.

<Stage path="prompt-input/loading" label="While the page loads" />

## Dictation

`PromptInputSpeechButton` starts a dictation, and while it listens the footer gives way to its bar: Cancel, the time it has run, the voice's `level` as a field of light that swells with the voice, the microphone (off for a moment with `muted`, when the time waits) and Done where Submit stood. Escape cancels, from the bar as from the text, and Enter sends nothing while the bar stands where Submit was. While it listens the words that were in the text before step back to the quiet colour, and what the dictation settles is drawn in ink. Words heard but not yet final go in `interim`: they show lighter after the text, and are no part of the message until the product writes them. Started from the keyboard, focus goes to Done and comes back to the button as the bar goes; a pointer's press leaves it in the text. The product listens, with the recogniser it chooses (the browser's own, a service), writes what it hears through the PromptInput's `controller`, and takes it out again in `onCancel`; the button holds no recognition of its own.

<Stage path="prompt-input/dictation" label="Dictation" />

```tsx
const [controller] = useState(createTokenInputController);
const [listening, setListening] = useState(false);
// The product's own recogniser, writing each phrase it hears into the text.
useDictation({ listening, onPhrase: (phrase) => controller.insert(phrase) });

<PromptInput controller={controller} onSubmit={send}>
  …
  <PromptInputSpeechButton
    listening={listening}
    onListeningChange={setListening}
    onCancel={takeOutWhatWasHeard}
    level={level}
  />
</PromptInput>;
```

## A spoken conversation

`PromptInputVoice` stands in the card while a spoken conversation runs, in place of the body and the footer: the words of the turn (those not yet final lighter, in `interim`), and under them the time, the voice's field, the microphone and a close button. The turns pass by themselves between the person (`status="listening"`) and the other voice (`speaking`: its mark, `speaker`, in the time's place, its field a stop quieter), each said once. The close button, or Escape, runs `onClose`; the product draws the text again, which takes the focus. The product listens and speaks.

<Stage path="prompt-input/voice" label="A spoken conversation" />

The field is one canvas: a body of the brand's light that swells with the voice in place. `sample` is the product's reading of the sound now, read at each frame with no render; `level` does for a value already in state. A noise gate, the speaker's own loudness, an envelope and a spring stand between reading and drawing. With reduced motion it is a still band; in forced colours it takes the system's text colour. Its code is fetched as the first voice starts, and `PromptInputSpeechButton` draws the same field. A stretch not made out is a `note` under the words.

What stops a voice is a `PromptInputBanner`: the card is back at its text, and the banner says why at once, with Try again where another go can work.

<Stage path="prompt-input/voice-problems" label="What stops a voice" />

## With a message

One chat state drives both. PromptInput's `status` takes the AI SDK's own (`ready`, `submitted`, `streaming`, `error`), and Message's describes one answer (`thinking`, `streaming`, `done`, `stopped`, `error`), so the latest answer's is read from it: `submitted` is `thinking`, `streaming` is `streaming`, `ready` is `done` (or `stopped` once the person stopped it), `error` is `error`. Every earlier answer is `done`. AI Elements has a status on PromptInput's Submit only.

```tsx
const { messages, status, sendMessage, stop } = useChat();
const [stopped, setStopped] = useState(false);
const answer: Record<ChatStatus, MessageStatus> = {
  submitted: "thinking",
  streaming: "streaming",
  ready: stopped ? "stopped" : "done",
  error: "error",
};

{
  messages.map((message, index) => (
    <Message
      key={message.id}
      align={message.role === "user" ? "end" : "start"}
      status={index === messages.length - 1 ? answer[status] : "done"}
    >
      …
    </Message>
  ));
}
<PromptInput
  status={status}
  onSubmit={({ text }) => {
    setStopped(false);
    // Not returned: it settles only once the whole answer has come.
    void sendMessage({ text });
  }}
>
  …
  <PromptInputSubmit
    onStop={() => {
      setStopped(true);
      void stop();
    }}
  />
</PromptInput>;
```
