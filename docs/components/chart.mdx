## Bars

`ChartContainer` holds the drawing; `ChartBar` draws each bar in the plain token colour. The arrow keys move between them.

<Stage path="chart/bar-plain" label="Bars" />

## Names that repeat

Two rows may share their name, a weekday over two weeks: each is its own bar and its own row in the chart's table, kept by its place, as the data changes too.

<Stage path="chart/same-names" label="Names that repeat" />

## Bars in pixels

`pattern="dither-pixels"` dithers the body in 2px cells; a solid cap carries the value.

<Stage path="chart/bar-pixels" label="Bars in pixels" />

## Bars in dots

`pattern="dither-dots"` uses fine 1px dots on a 3px pitch instead.

<Stage path="chart/bar-dots" label="Bars in dots" />

## Bars across

`ChartBarList` lays kinds side by side as bars across, largest first. Each name is read where its bar starts and wraps by the word on a phone; each value stands at the row's end. No axis: the words carry the numbers.

<Stage path="chart/bar-list" label="Bars across" />

## Bars across in pixels

`pattern="dither-pixels"` lights the dither at each bar's tip, with a solid cap saying the value.

<Stage path="chart/bar-list-pixels" label="Bars across in pixels" />

## Bars across in dots

The fine dots, lit at each tip the same way.

<Stage path="chart/bar-list-dots" label="Bars across in dots" />

## This year against last

A second series in the config is drawn as a muted bar under the first, and named on the first row instead of in a legend: beside the values, or above each bar on a narrow chart.

<Stage path="chart/bar-list-compare" label="This year against last" />

## Below zero

A value below zero runs left from a faint zero line; the names stay where the row starts. `valueFormatter` writes the value, with a real minus sign (−), not a hyphen.

<Stage path="chart/bar-list-change" label="Below zero" />

## A line

`ChartLine` draws a trend as the line alone, in the value colour, with nothing under it.

<Stage path="chart/line" label="A line" />

## Lines compared

Several `ChartLine`s share one chart. Each takes a `variant` (`solid`, `dashed`, `dotted`, `thin`), so the lines are told apart without colour, and `ChartLabels` names each at its end in place of a legend; where two end close together, their names step apart. Hover shows a point on every line and one tooltip, highest first (`sortOrder="descending"`). Five lines at most: past that, a table or one chart per line reads better.

<Stage path="chart/lines-several" label="Lines compared" />

## With a legend

`ChartLegend` names each series under the chart, by its name in the config, beside a swatch of the line.

<Stage path="chart/legend" label="With a legend" />

## This period against last

`variant="muted"` draws the earlier period muted, thinner and dashed, under the value.

<Stage path="chart/lines-compare" label="This period against last" />

## A ring

`ChartPie` shows the parts of a whole round a ring, the total in its centre with one line of words (`caption`). Each part is a series in the config, in order from twelve o'clock, with a clear gap between. The names live in a quiet list beside the ring (under it on a narrow chart), never hung round it. Hover or focus a part and it lifts a little while the centre says it.

<Stage path="chart/ring" label="A ring" />

## A ring in pixels

`pattern="dither-pixels"` dithers the first part, lit at its outer edge, with a solid cap along it. The rest step down the chart tones.

<Stage path="chart/ring-pixels" label="A ring in pixels" />

## A ring in dots

The fine dots on the first part.

<Stage path="chart/ring-dots" label="A ring in dots" />

## Five parts

Five parts at most: past that, bars across say it better. Write every value to the same number of decimals (`€14.0K`, not `€14K` under `€24.1K`), so the column reads as one precision and no value looks like a mistake.

<Stage path="chart/ring-list" label="Five parts" />

## One share

One value and a `total` it is part of: the share in the centre, the rest of the ring a faint empty track.

<Stage path="chart/ring-share" label="One share" />

## A spark in a table

`ChartSparkline` draws a trend small enough to sit beside its number: a line in the value colour, 20px tall, with no axis, grid or words inside it. The number beside it says the value; its `aria-label` and `summary` say the trend to a screen reader. `showHighlight="last"` puts one dot on the last point. In a table it is a picture, never a control.

<Stage path="chart/spark-table" label="A spark in a table" />

## A spark in a highlight

`size="lg"` stands 44px tall under a highlight's figure (its `chart` prop), with a soft `area` if asked. Given `onHighlightChange`, the pointer or the arrow keys pick a week and the tile's own figure says it; no tooltip opens.

<Stage path="chart/spark-tile" label="A spark in a highlight" />

## Every shape

Rising, falling, flat, noisy, crossing zero and a single point, at both sizes. Flat and one-point series draw a straight line; a faint zero line shows only where a series crosses zero; a wobble of a few per cent stays nearly flat. No dither at this size, and no draw-in.

<Stage path="chart/spark-shapes" label="Every shape" />

## Flows between stages

`ChartSankey` shows how a whole moves between stages: each node as tall as what passes through it, each flow a ribbon as thick as its value, from its source's tone to its target's. Columns come from the longest path, and nodes are ordered in their column to cut crossings. A `muted` node is an end not hoped for (disputed, waiting): its ribbons fade to muted. Hover or Tab to a flow and its whole path lights while the rest steps back; a line under the drawing says its value and its share of where it came from, so nothing covers the flows. The ribbons draw in once, left to right; on a narrow chart the flows become a list by stage.

<Stage path="chart/sankey" label="Flows between stages" />

## Flows with dithered nodes

`pattern="dither-pixels"` dithers the nodes, lit at the top. The dither is a fill: the thickness carries the hours.

<Stage path="chart/sankey-hours" label="Flows with dithered nodes" />
