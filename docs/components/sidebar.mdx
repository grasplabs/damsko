## Basic usage

The parts: a SidebarProvider holds whether it is open and the row it stands in, the Sidebar beside a SidebarInset, the page. In the sidebar a SidebarHeader (here the product's mark, a search as a SidebarMenuButton of `size="icon"`, and the SidebarTrigger), a SidebarContent of SidebarGroups, and a SidebarSeparator over the SidebarFooter, where a large SidebarMenuButton is the person's menu. A group holds a SidebarGroupLabel over a SidebarMenu of SidebarMenuItems, each a SidebarMenuButton, a link through `asChild`, with a SidebarMenuBadge or a SidebarMenuSub beside it. `isActive` fills the page you are on. The sidebar stands in the page's flow, never fixed to the window, and stays in view while the page scrolls. The trigger, or Cmd+B (Ctrl+B elsewhere), folds it away whole.

<Stage path="sidebar/basic" label="Basic usage" />

## Off the canvas

The default, `collapsible="offcanvas"`: folded, it slides out past its side, the page takes the width and nothing in it is a tab stop. A SidebarRail along its edge folds and opens it too. `keyboardShortcut` changes the keys, `false` turns them off.

<Stage path="sidebar/collapsible" label="Off the canvas" />

## Folded to its icons

`collapsible="icon"` folds it to a rail of its icons instead, where each row shows its words in a tooltip (`tooltip` says something else there). The rows within a place go while it is folded. A `disabled` button is drawn at half strength.

<Stage path="sidebar/icon" label="Folded to its icons" />

## Long names

A row's words are its bare text: longer than the row, they end in an ellipsis before the row's end, its count or a mark it holds, and the row's tooltip shows them whole. An element you write in a row is drawn as given.

<Stage path="sidebar/long-names" label="Long names" />

## Floating and inset

`variant="floating"` raises it a little in from the edges; `variant="inset"` leaves the page as a card on the sidebar's ground.

<Stage path="sidebar/floating" label="Floating" />

<Stage path="sidebar/inset" label="Inset" />

## At the end

`side="end"`, after the SidebarInset. Start and end follow the reading direction.

<Stage path="sidebar/side" label="At the end" />

## Groups

A Collapsible folds a group from its label: its trigger is drawn as the SidebarGroupLabel with `asChild`. A SidebarGroupAction stands at the end of the label; a SidebarInput in the header filters the rows.

<Stage path="sidebar/groups" label="Groups" />

## A row's actions

A SidebarMenuAction at the end of a row opens its menu, shown while the pointer or focus is on the row with `showOnHover`, and always on a touch screen. Whatever the product marks a row with, such as an agent's working indicator, goes in the button's children.

<Stage path="sidebar/actions" label="A row's actions" />

## The foot

The footer holds what the product composes there: the theme control, then the person signed in, a large SidebarMenuButton as their menu's trigger. A SidebarSeparator draws the rule over it.

<Stage path="sidebar/footer" label="The foot" />

## Before the rows arrive

SidebarMenuSkeleton draws quiet bars in the rows' own boxes, the same shapes every time. They are hidden from a screen reader: the menu says it is busy.

<Stage path="sidebar/states" label="Before the rows arrive" />

## Kept as the person left it

`storageKey` keeps the open state in localStorage. `sidebarStateScript(storageKey)` in the page's head draws it before the page wakes, so nothing moves. Or keep it yourself with `open` and `onOpenChange`, from a cookie read on the server.

<Stage path="sidebar/persisted" label="Kept as the person left it" />

## From the page

`useSidebar()` reads and sets the open state from anywhere inside the provider: a page's own control folds it with `setOpen`, which takes a value or an updater, as React's `setState`.

<Stage path="sidebar/from-the-page" label="From the page" />

## Levels

An area with a navigation of its own, such as settings or one project, takes the sidebar while the person is in it. SidebarLevels stands between the header and the footer, which stay, and holds a SidebarLevel for each area, the main list first. `value` says which is shown: keep it where the address is kept, and the rows are plain links. A deeper level starts with a SidebarLevelHeader, a SidebarMenuButton that leads back and the SidebarLevelTitle, and each level has its own SidebarContent, named by `aria-label`. A sidebar that keeps the level itself takes `defaultValue`, and a SidebarLevelTrigger, a SidebarMenuButton that also shows a level, changes it.

The level left stays in the page, unseen and out of reach, with its scroll place and its folded groups, so keep a level's rows cheap. The sheet on a narrow window is drawn anew each time it opens, on the level shown. Folded to its icons, the title is its icon alone.

Use a level for a different area with its own navigation; the pages within one place are a SidebarMenuSub under its row.

[App shell](/components/app-shell-01#levels) shows it live, the address choosing the level.
